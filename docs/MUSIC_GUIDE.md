# Collaborative Music Creation Guide

Last updated: 2026-08-24

## Purpose And Operating Promise

This is the durable operating guide for making emotionally purposeful,
memorable, original music with the user. Its job is to explain how to think
with everything we know about music, not to store every technique in one
document or force every piece through one formula.

The guide owns the universal reasoning process:

- understand the musical job;
- listen before explaining;
- separate discovery from diagnosis;
- preserve selected and approved work;
- identify the earliest broken musical decision;
- assemble compatible methods for the current context;
- make the smallest useful audible experiment;
- interpret listening evidence honestly; and
- return durable learning at the correct scope.

Detailed conditional knowledge may live in the private music wiki under
`Wiki/Knowledge/Music/`. Project approvals, taste, history, implementation,
and technical constraints remain in their project folders. Reviewed research
and knowledge gaps remain in `docs/music/RESEARCH_LIBRARY.md`.

The guide must still work when the private wiki is unavailable. In that case,
use this document, supplied material, the research library when present, and
applicable project sources. Build a compact working brief, label missing
knowledge, and do not invent a method or claim that the unavailable wiki
contains.

This is a method, not a recipe. Genre convention, theory, production
technique, and narrative intention are possibilities to judge through musical
cause and listening evidence.

## Enter The Work

### Establish Scope

Before using project history, decide whether the request is project-attached.
A request is project-attached when the user names the project or artifact,
supplies material with clear project provenance, continues an established
project decision, or asks about current implementation, approval, or history.

When the request is project-neutral:

- use this general guide and supplied material only;
- do not import project taste, cue history, approvals, mechanics, or technical
  constraints merely because they exist in the workspace;
- ask one scope question only when project identity would materially change
  the answer; and
- scan the final response for leaked project names or assumptions.

When the request is project-attached:

1. Read the current project brief and approval or lifecycle source.
2. Read worklogs only when exact history matters.
3. Inspect the current artifact or implementation when a claim depends on it.
4. Separate selected, approved, exploratory, rejected, superseded, completed,
   and shipped material.
5. Preserve the last approved or completed version before experimenting.

Reconcile direction in this order: the newest explicit user correction,
active continuity state, current project authority, then historical evidence.
State material conflicts instead of silently protecting an older idea.

### Evidence And Status

A musical conclusion is only as broad as the evidence that produced it.
Distinguish:

- listener evidence;
- personal preference;
- project identity;
- genre convention;
- research evidence;
- experiment result;
- hypothesis;
- selected component;
- approved work;
- completed deliverable; and
- shipped implementation.

An audition is not approved because it exists. A technically stable render is
not musically approved. A successful short sketch does not approve a complete
form. One project's taste does not become universal doctrine.

When the private wiki is available, consult
`Wiki/Knowledge/Music/Foundations/Evidence Scope and Musical Status.md` for the
expanded evidence model.

## Listen Before Explaining

The user does not need technical vocabulary to provide precise evidence.
Descriptions such as "it feels like the music stopped," "I wanted it to keep
going," "the handoff feels fake," or "this is impressive but I feel nothing"
are musically useful.

Respond by:

1. locating the exact moment the response changed;
2. identifying a small number of plausible causes;
3. explaining them in plain language;
4. testing the earliest plausible cause through sound; and
5. revising the diagnosis when listening contradicts intention.

Do not explain the intended emotion before the listener has a chance to react.
If the music needs a specific emotional message, it should communicate enough
of that message without a label. Ambiguity remains valid when it is the goal.

Distinguish:

- immediate physical or emotional reaction;
- response after the form becomes familiar;
- fatigue or attachment across repeated listening;
- the intended audience's context; and
- whether the music succeeds at its actual job.

Novelty can win a first listen and fail over time. Familiarity can also protect
a weaker control. Important decisions should survive more than one exposure.

## Choose The Working Mode

Use the smallest mode that fits the decision.

- **Direction:** define purpose, identity, references, or next steps.
- **Identity:** discover or refine a motif, hook, groove, cadence, harmonic
  gesture, timbral fingerprint, or performance attitude.
- **Composition:** turn material into phrases, sections, and form.
- **Diagnosis:** find the earliest likely cause of a failed response.
- **Arrangement and production:** clarify hierarchy, motion, sound, dynamics,
  space, and mix after identity is viable.
- **Interactive music:** design loops, layers, transitions, states, stingers,
  and synchronization.
- **Technical audio:** reproduce and repair playback defects without silently
  redesigning the music.

### Discovery

Discovery may begin with melody, rhythm, harmony, lyric, bass, sound design,
performance, field recording, emotional image, or structural accident.
Several variables may move together when their interaction is the identity.

Generate enough materially different possibilities to reveal choice. Preserve
accidents that feel alive. Do not polish the first workable idea before it has
a memorable relationship or appetite for continuation.

### Diagnosis

Diagnosis begins after something is worth preserving. Name the question,
retain an exact control, hold irrelevant variables constant, and change one
meaningful variable or one coordinated causal hypothesis.

Controlled comparison is a learning tool, not a restriction on the final
piece. Do not solve an identity problem with production polish or a form
problem with more layers.

## Build The Musical Brief

Start with the smallest brief that can orient the next musical decision.
Always identify:

- project-attached or project-neutral scope;
- musical job or current problem;
- listener and listening context;
- existing material and identity;
- selected or approved material that must be protected;
- creative and technical constraints; and
- the current question.

Add duration, repetition, attention, emotional change, cultural context,
delivery, masking, and reference qualities only when they affect the decision.

A useful emotional north star is:

> The listener begins here, passes through this change, and should want or feel
> this by the end.

It is optional. A concrete musical objective may be stronger for diagnosis,
technical work, or material-led composition. Any north star may be revised
when the music reveals a stronger truth.

## Assemble An Approach Bundle

An approach bundle is a temporary, task-specific hierarchy of profiles,
methods, evidence, exclusions, and an audible decision contract. It is not a
preset arrangement.

Use a bundle only when selecting among methods, reconciling conflicts, or
preserving reasoning will improve the decision. A simple direction or diagnosis
may need only the brief.

### Choose The Entry Path

- **Brief-led:** use when composing from an open page or when the function and
  constraints must shape the first sketch.
- **Material-led:** use when a melody, groove, sound, performance, or accident
  already has life. First identify what the material is doing, what creates its
  appetite, and what must not be explained away. Then select profiles and
  methods around that evidence.

The bundle is provisional. Revise it after the first meaningful sound when the
music contradicts the plan.

### Select Independent Profiles

Consider only the axes relevant to the brief:

- **Genre:** conventions, vocabulary, production expectations, and
  alternatives.
- **Scene function:** what the music does in a story, system, performance, or
  game.
- **Format:** song, loop, adaptive cue, linear score, live piece, recording,
  or another delivery form.
- **Emotional or character role:** identity, relationship, transformation,
  perspective, or dramatic status.
- **Technical context:** performers, tools, code, middleware, playback, room,
  and delivery constraints.

Keep these axes separate. EDM does not imply a Boss Fight. A Boss Fight does
not imply fast electronic music. A loop does not require low emotional range.

When the private wiki is available, begin at
`Wiki/Knowledge/Music/Music Knowledge Map.md`. Retrieve the smallest relevant
set of notes rather than loading the entire library.

### Build A Method Hierarchy

Choose:

1. **One primary method** that most directly causes the desired musical
   result, or one tightly coupled method cluster when the interaction itself is
   the hypothesis.
2. **A small number of supporting methods** with distinct jobs.
3. **Deliberately excluded methods** that are tempting but incompatible,
   unsupported, premature, or already rejected.

For every selected method, check:

- when it applies;
- when to avoid it;
- prerequisites;
- compatibility with the other methods;
- conflicts;
- failure modes;
- evidence quality and confidence;
- project fit; and
- what the listener should hear if it works.

Do not average conflicting techniques into a vague compromise. Preserve them
as alternatives when the current evidence cannot select between them.

Stop retrieving notes when the primary cause, support, exclusions, and material
risks can be justified. More context is not automatically better judgment.

When the current question is answered, mark the bundle `resolved`. When a newer
bundle replaces it, mark it `archived` and link its successor. Do not leave
answered bundles looking active.

### Composer Review

Before recommending the bundle, challenge the obvious answer:

1. What job must the music perform, for whom, and for how long?
2. What should the listener feel first, and how should that change?
3. Which existing element carries the strongest identity or appetite?
4. What is the earliest musical decision that appears broken?
5. What is selected or approved and must remain intact?
6. Would this direction still be compelling if the user had not proposed it?
7. Does each method cause the intended result or merely add activity?
8. Is a simpler hierarchy stronger?
9. What could fail through fatigue, repetition, ambiguity, weak preparation,
   or technical limits?
10. What is the smallest audible experiment that can resolve the uncertainty?

Ask no more than three questions when subjective intent genuinely remains
necessary. Resolve discoverable facts from the available evidence. Record only
the answers that materially change the brief, bundle, or experiment.

## Develop The Music

Use this cycle flexibly. Move backward when a later failure exposes an earlier
weakness.

### Generate Material

Create enough material to reveal choice: a motif, phrase, beat, chord loop,
lyric, bass motion, timbre, texture, performance gesture, or small form.

Look for:

- something memorable after it stops;
- a gesture that wants continuation;
- a sound or rhythm with personality;
- a useful emotional contradiction;
- a phrase whose ending creates appetite; or
- an accidental relationship more specific than the plan.

Complexity is not identity. One strong relationship can carry more character
than many unrelated details.

### Recognize Identity

Identity may live in:

- contour and rhythm;
- an interval or chord behavior;
- groove or pocket;
- lyric hook and prosody;
- timbral fingerprint;
- performance attitude;
- bass and melody relationship;
- cadence or destination;
- tension and release; or
- recurring use of space.

Protect two or three anchors while allowing everything else to develop.
Theory names relationships and enables variation; it does not prove emotion.

For familiar material entering a new scene, separate:

1. **Ancestry:** what must remain recognizable?
2. **Scene identity:** what new hook, cadence, groove, pressure engine,
   attitude, or purpose makes the new cue necessary?

Callbacks should perform different jobs, not compete as equal quotations.
Test recognition without labels and test the new cue with callbacks reduced.

### Develop Through Time

Repetition teaches the listener what matters. Development changes what a
return accomplishes.

Options include:

- fragmentation and recombination;
- sequence or transposition;
- rhythmic compression, expansion, or displacement;
- extension and delayed completion;
- changed harmony or bass;
- changed cadence or destination;
- counterpoint;
- changed register or orchestration;
- subtraction and exposed space;
- role transfer; and
- a new relationship among established ideas.

Timbral change and phrase development are different. A brighter repeat can
refresh a texture while still performing the same stale function.

Give every large section a reason to exist: establish, deepen, question,
accelerate, release, destabilize, recover, transform, or prepare return.
Movement length should follow the musical sentence and dramatic job rather
than a uniform grid.

### Character, Relationship, And Authority

An ordinary, comic, gentle, fragile, or minor identity can gain consequence
through phrase authority, cadence, bass and harmonic weight, register,
foreground ownership, ensemble scale, interruption, restraint, or the right
to finish material.

Preserve enough original behavior that transformation reinterprets the
identity instead of replacing it with generic intensity.

Developmental reserve is optional. An early statement may withhold a beat,
answer, register, bass, harmony, or destination so a later statement can fill,
extend, combine, or redirect it. Use reserve only when the later completion
matters. Do not force every piece through a confidence ladder.

Musical identities may overlap, interrupt, move in parallel, leave room,
finish one another's phrases, or selectively borrow traits. Literal
alternating dialogue is only one option.

### Rhythm, Harmony, And Arrangement

Rhythm organizes attention. Harmony organizes expectation. Arrangement
determines which idea the listener can follow.

Energy may change through subdivision, accent, groove, harmonic rhythm, bass
motion, articulation, phrase turnover, register, density, ensemble size,
brightness, width, depth, space, stability, and performance intensity. Tempo
and loudness are only two dimensions.

Harmony gains meaning from order, voicing, bass, harmonic rhythm, register,
dissonance, melody, and destination. Major and minor are not complete
emotional descriptions.

Choose a focal element at each moment. Supporting roles may provide pulse,
bass, harmony, counterline, texture, transition, impact, or atmosphere. A
layer should add audible value worth the attention and frequency space it
uses.

Impact is relational. An arrival works because the preceding music left
something meaningful to reclaim: low end, width, brightness, density,
register, rhythmic weight, dryness, intimacy, ensemble scale, stability, or
thematic completion.

### Sound, Performance, Lyrics, And Mix

Sound design, orchestration, and performance are compositional decisions.
Envelope, filtering, articulation, dynamics, tuning movement, pan, space,
touch, breath, bowing, register, and instrumental combination can change what
a phrase means.

Subtle modulation may keep repetition alive. Obvious modulation becomes a
foreground gesture and should be composed as one.

For vocals, consider central image, perspective, natural word stress, vowels,
range, tessitura, breath, phonetics, performer identity, and whether the
arrangement leaves the voice intelligible. Instrumental music also benefits
from vocal thinking: breath, sentence shape, attack, sustain, and a sense of
who is speaking.

Establish a rough mix early enough to hear the composition honestly. Begin
with arrangement, sound choice, faders, and pan. Processing cannot reliably
rescue unclear identity or purposeless form.

Compare revisions at matched perceived loudness. Check low volume, mono,
headphones, speakers, relevant real systems, and hearing fatigue.

## Diagnose The Earliest Broken Decision

Fix the earliest cause rather than decorating the symptom.

| Listener evidence | First areas to test |
|---|---|
| Needs more energy | Subdivision, groove, harmony, bass, articulation, phrase turnover, contrast, then tempo |
| Needs to feel bigger | Preparation, arrival, low end, register, ensemble scale, width, or thematic completion |
| Feels repetitive | Phrase function, harmony, rhythm, exposure, destination, then timbre |
| Transition feels fake | Preparation, voice leading, groove, role ownership, destination quality |
| Busy but boring | Identity, focal hierarchy, consequence, contrast |
| Melody feels stunted | Contour, approach motion, leap purpose, phrase destination |
| Low section feels empty | Pulse, harmony, texture, bass, or restrained focal thread |
| Faster still feels calm | Accent, subdivision, harmonic rhythm, bass, performance, phrase compression |
| Mix is impressive but music is weak | Return to simple sound and test material, hierarchy, and form |
| Emotion needs explanation | Simplify the intended turn and capture an unprompted reaction |
| Static, click, or dropout | Reproduce, isolate, timestamp, and repair the technical cause |

This table suggests where to listen. It does not prove the cause.

## Build The Smallest Audible Experiment

Use a discovery audition for materially different identities or directions.
Use a diagnostic audition for a specific causal question.

### Audition Contract

For every substantive recommendation that selects, diagnoses, or claims
readiness, state:

- exact control;
- held variables;
- changed variable or coordinated hypothesis;
- audible decision criterion;
- what each plausible result changes next;
- repeated complete-phrase or complete-form exposure; and
- current status plus remaining subjective, technical, delivery, and
  integration gates.

Use neutral candidate names before the first listen. Match perceived loudness
unless loudness is the variable. Provide replay, pause, seek, timestamps, and
direct switching when the medium supports them.

Remove obsolete candidates from focused listening surfaces while preserving
approved references and useful history.

### Technical Verification

For code-generated or interactive auditions, verify:

- syntax and reproducibility;
- complete phrases and forms;
- multiple uninterrupted loops;
- first play, stop, and replay;
- pause and resume;
- seeking across edited boundaries;
- rapid switching;
- long-playback stability;
- bounded sources and schedulers;
- cleanup after stop, seek, and switch;
- clipping, static, clicks, dropouts, and effect accumulation;
- usable low-volume signal and maximum-volume headroom;
- candidate-level fairness;
- runtime or browser errors; and
- required transport and monitoring controls.

A nonzero analyser reading is not an audibility standard. Technical success
proves delivery and comparison stability, not composition quality.

## Listen And Interpret Evidence

Hear complete phrases or forms, not only the edited second. For loops, hear
multiple uninterrupted returns. Compare at a repeatable level and manually
restart when diagnosing state buildup.

Capture the unprompted reaction before explaining the hypothesis. Then compare
intention with effect.

Keep taste and defect separate:

- dislike of a sound is valid taste evidence;
- a reproducible click, dropout, distorted render, or missing voice is a
  defect; and
- a one-off artifact needs reproduction before it drives composition changes.

State what the result proves, what it does not prove, and the next
highest-information question.

## Format-Specific Questions

### Songs

Prioritize hook identity, lyric perspective, prosody, vocal range, section
contrast, performer connection, and a form that deepens the central statement
on return.

### Instrumental Music And Score

Prioritize motif, dramatic function, orchestration, pacing, thematic
transformation, and direction of attention without words.

### Interactive And Game Music

Define scene function, listening duration, attention, loop seam, fatigue,
horizontal transitions, vertical layers, stingers, parameters, sync points,
masking, and relationships among states.

State changes should be musically meaningful, not merely louder or denser.
Reduced passages should retain a deliberate pulse, harmony, texture, or thread
unless silence has a dramatic purpose.

Compose endings against beginnings. Layers may repeat on different time scales
to create stability and change. Test exact repetition, variation, state
transitions, and long exposure separately.

### Generative Or Code-Based Music

Treat scheduling, state, cleanup, randomness, reproducibility, and performance
limits as part of the composition. Keep project-specific architecture and
tests in the project folder.

### Live And Recorded Performance

Consider range, idiomatic writing, notation, rehearsal, room, microphones,
phase, noise, editing philosophy, and how much imperfection carries the
intended humanity.

## Use References And Research Responsibly

Extract qualities such as emotional arc, tempo behavior, phrase motion,
orchestration, density, harmony, groove, register, production perspective, or
interactive function. Do not copy protected melody, lyrics, arrangement,
signature sound design, performance, or recording.

When an admired creator is named, treat the name as a standard of craft.
Identify transferable principles and create original expression from the
brief, identity, and material.

When requested similarity is too close, decline replication and return to a
source-independent identity decision or focused audition.

Research only when a concrete knowledge gap, unfamiliar practice, unresolved
diagnosis, technical risk, or promising direction could change the musical
decision.

1. State the research question.
2. Prefer primary sources and directly reviewed expert material.
3. Use multiple perspectives for contextual or contested claims.
4. Separate supported principle, convention, marketing, uncertain caption,
   and opinion.
5. Record provenance, findings, limits, and confidence.
6. Integrate only durable learning that improves future judgment.
7. Preserve conflicts by context rather than silently replacing one method.

Interesting information alone is not enough to change the guide or wiki.

## Maintain The Second Brain

The guide owns the universal decision process. The private wiki may store:

- foundations and evidence scope;
- genre, scene-function, format, character-role, and technical profiles;
- conditional methods;
- source records;
- analyses;
- synthesized knowledge;
- listening evidence;
- project cases; and
- temporary approach bundles.

When adding knowledge:

1. Preserve source provenance.
2. Separate source description from analysis and synthesis.
3. Label confidence and scope.
4. Record applies-when, avoid-when, prerequisites, conflicts, and failure
   modes for methods.
5. Keep project evidence out of universal claims.
6. Link rather than copy full note contents into bundles.
7. Revise an existing note when the central claim survives.
8. Preserve conflicting valid methods when their contexts differ.
9. Do not fill folders merely to appear comprehensive.

An approach bundle is temporary project reasoning. Promote only durable
learning, and only at the scope the evidence supports.

If the private wiki is absent, use the working contract below in the task or
continuity record. Do not make permanent documentation unless the user asks or
the project workflow requires it.

## Finish And Deliver

Before final delivery, confirm:

- the complete work, not only one section, is approved;
- identity and emotional purpose survive repeated listening;
- transitions, endings, and loop points are intentional;
- the mix translates at the intended volume and systems;
- technical artifacts are resolved;
- loudness, headroom, fades, metadata, formats, stems, and alternates match the
  destination;
- samples, references, performances, and assets are legally usable;
- the approved master is identifiable; and
- open questions are answered or consciously accepted.

Finishing is a creative decision. Endless revision can erase character. A new
experiment must not destroy the version that already worked.

## Communicate The Recommendation

For a substantial review, report:

1. **Verdict:** the recommendation in plain language.
2. **Composer questions:** the important questions and concise answers.
3. **Reservations:** critical problems, tuning watchpoints, and matters of
   taste.
4. **Approach bundle:** profiles, primary method, support, exclusions, and
   compatibility.
5. **Next audition:** the smallest comparison that can teach us something.
6. **Decision status:** exact evidence and approval scope.

Explain technical language through what the listener will hear. Be candid when
music is pleasant but unfocused, impressive but emotionally flat, or
overworked around weak material.
